# Player psychology: use it kindly

Each principle has three parts: what it is, how to use it here, and the line
not to cross. The test for every one is the same. **Would the member thank us
for this if they saw exactly how it works?** If not, don't build it.

## Motivation and reward

**Overjustification effect.** Paying people for something they already
enjoy turns it into work. When the pay stops, they do it less than before.
- **Use:** keep the popoto a gesture. Celebrate kindness ("12 people thanked
  you this week") rather than paying for it.
- **Don't:** attach growing prizes to giving popoto, or the gratitude
  becomes farming. The prize draws and Evercold already sit near this line:
  keep their value small and fun, and never make them the reason to give.

**Variable reward** (variable-ratio schedules). Unpredictable rewards are
exciting: the rare popoto works because of this.
- **Use:** as a surprise layered on an action people do anyway, with the
  odds shown.
- **Don't:** build an action that exists only to roll the dice, add paid
  rolls, or stage near-misses.

**Goal-gradient effect.** People speed up as a goal gets close.
- **Use:** progress bars for collections or event goals that start with some
  progress ("2/7 found").
- **Don't:** fake the progress.

**Endowed progress.** People are more likely to finish something that
already looks started.
- **Use:** an onboarding checklist that ticks off what is already done
  ("character verified ✓").
- **Don't:** add invented steps.

**Zeigarnik effect.** Unfinished tasks stay in mind.
- **Use:** a gentle "your look is a draft" on your own profile.
- **Don't:** use open loops in notifications to nag.

**Completion and collection.** Sets beg to be completed (flavours, badges,
mounts).
- **Use:** collections with visible slots, and with friends' help
  (trading, gifting).
- **Don't:** use a paid or impossibly rare final piece, or a timed-exclusive
  "last chance" for things that matter.

**Competence feedback.** Clear, immediate signals of getting better.
- **Use:** "your first party as lead!", visible streaks of help given,
  grades on the playstyle boards.
- **Don't:** use vanity numbers that only go up.

## Social

**Reciprocity.** Receiving makes people want to give back. It is the engine
of the popoto ("throw back" from the bell).
- **Use:** make returning a kindness one tap.
- **Don't:** use guilt ("X sent you 5 and you sent 0").

**Social proof.** People do what they see others do.
- **Use:** "14 members entered the contest", a live dot on the gallery tab,
  "3 friends are in Cash Town".
- **Don't:** fake activity, or quote numbers that shame.

**Belonging and identity.** "We, the FC": the shared jokes (🥔), the
restaurant ranks, the FC's own words.
- **Use:** team goals, FC-wide celebrations, rituals.
- **Don't:** draw an in-group and out-group inside the FC (guests and
  vacationers are family too).

**Status and recognition.** Being seen as good at something, or as kind.
- **Use:** many kinds of status (helper, collector, decorator, photographer,
  raider) and status earned through service.
- **Don't:** use a single ranking that only a few can top.

**Cooperation over competition.** Positive-sum play builds community, and
zero-sum play strains it.
- **Use:** FC goals, helping friends' farms, group photos.
- **Don't:** use PvP over things people care about. Competition is opt-in,
  friendly and low-stakes.

## Perception and memory

**Peak-end rule.** People remember an experience by its best moment and its
end.
- **Use:** make the peak a real peak (the rare reveal, the podium), and end
  events with a warm recap ("this Halloween in numbers").
- **Don't:** end on a loss screen.

**Novelty and habituation.** Delight fades when repeated.
- **Use:** rotate seasonal skins, flavours and themes. Keep the rarest
  moments rare.
- **Don't:** shout everywhere, so that nothing stands out.

**Anticipation.** Waiting can be part of the pleasure (a wrapped popoto, a
countdown to the reveal).
- **Use:** short, chosen waits, like "tap to unwrap".
- **Don't:** impose timers to slow people down or to sell skips.

**IKEA effect.** People value what they built.
- **Use:** homes, showcases, farms, profiles. Let members make things.
- **Don't:** take away what they built when they're away.

**Endowment and ownership.** Things feel more valuable once they're "mine".
- **Use:** "my popoto", "my pet", "my house".
- **Don't:** threaten to take them away (decay, loss) to force a return.

## The ones to handle with care

**Loss aversion.** Losses hurt about twice as much as gains please.
- **Use:** mainly to protect people. Make sure nothing they own can be lost
  to an absence or a rival.
- **Don't:** use streak-loss threats, decaying rewards, or "your pet is
  sad" guilt.

**Scarcity.** Rare things feel more valuable.
- **Use:** genuinely rare cosmetics, plainly described.
- **Don't:** use fake "only today!" urgency, or limited-time items that
  matter.

**Sunk cost.** People keep going because of what they already put in.
- **Never** design for it.

**Near-miss.** Almost winning feels like being close to a win, and drives
gambling.
- **Never** show a staged near-miss in a draw.

## Dark patterns we refuse

- Confirmshaming ("No thanks, I don't like my friends").
- Forced continuity, or nagging notifications.
- Hidden or misleading odds.
- Fake scarcity or fake activity.
- Streak hostage-taking.
- Leaderboards of shame.
- Pay-to-win, or any real-money purchase.
- Gacha with money.
- Infinite scroll built to trap people.
- Autoplay that keeps going.
- Making it hard to leave or to hide yourself.

If a mechanic needs one of these to work, the mechanic is wrong.

## Who might be hurt

- **Members with less time:** work, family, a timezone. Design so that 2
  minutes a week is a full membership.
- **Members having a hard time:** no public "you've been inactive" signals.
- **People prone to compulsive play:** keep the odds visible, keep the
  stakes small, and never use money.
- **Quiet members:** nothing exposes them. Opt in to the spotlight.
