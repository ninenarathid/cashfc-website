# Frameworks: the lenses game designers use

Each section gives the idea, then how it reads on this site. Use them as
lenses, not laws: the point is to ask better questions.

## 1. MDA

**Mechanics** are the rules we write (one popoto per person per day). They
produce **dynamics**, which is what players actually do (people throw back
at whoever threw to them). Those produce **aesthetics**, which is what
players feel (being thought of, warmth).

- Designers build from mechanics up. Players experience the game from
  aesthetics down.
- So start from the feeling you want and work backwards. Then watch the
  dynamics that really appear, because they are often not the ones intended.

On this site: the rule "one popoto per pair per day" produced a daily
ritual of mutual greeting. That dynamic is the real product, so protect it.

## 2. The eight kinds of fun (LeBlanc)

| Kind | Feeling | Where it lives here |
|---|---|---|
| Sensation | the senses pleased | the throw animation, the rare reveal, foil badges |
| Fantasy | make-believe | the restaurant theme, popoto as a character |
| Narrative | a story unfolding | prog through a tier, a contest from entries to the podium |
| Challenge | obstacles overcome | raids (in-game), the M9S guide quiz, collection boards |
| Fellowship | together | parties, cheering, the gallery, comments |
| Discovery | the unknown explored | rare flavours and their lines, hidden things |
| Expression | showing who you are | glamours, profile colour, bio, showcase |
| Submission | pastime, unwinding | scrolling the gallery, a farm you tend for a minute |

A feature should pick one or two **on purpose**. "Fun" without a kind is
nothing in particular.

## 3. Self-determination theory (Deci and Ryan)

People keep doing things that feed three needs:
- **Autonomy:** "I chose this." Options, self-expression, opting in, no
  coercion.
- **Competence:** "I'm getting better, and it shows." Clear feedback,
  visible progress, a challenge that fits.
- **Relatedness:** "I matter to these people." Being seen, thanked,
  included, needed.

Relatedness is this site's superpower: every system that works here (the
popoto, tags, parties, birthdays) is relatedness. Rewards that are felt as
*controlling* (do X to get Y) undermine autonomy, and with it the motivation
that was there before (see the overjustification effect in psychology.md).

## 4. Player motivations

**Bartle's four** (from MUDs, which are the ancestors of Cash Town):
- Achievers: points, completion.
- Explorers: secrets, how it works.
- Socializers: people.
- Killers: acting on others.

The last should become "competitors", in friendly forms only. Most players
are a mix. An FC hangout leans socializer, with a strong achiever streak
(raids, collections).

**Quantic Foundry** (more modern, from surveys of over a million gamers):
six motivations in pairs:
- action (destruction, excitement);
- social (community, competition);
- mastery (challenge, strategy);
- achievement (completion, power);
- immersion (fantasy, story);
- creativity (design, discovery).

Use them to check coverage. A plan made only of competition and completion
leaves out the community, design and fantasy players, who are often the
quiet majority of an FC.

## 5. Flow (Csikszentmihalyi)

Flow is deep enjoyment when the challenge matches the skill, goals are clear
and feedback is immediate. Too hard brings anxiety; too easy brings boredom.

- For a website, "flow" is mostly frictionless feedback. Every press
  answers at once, and progress is visible.
- For a mini-game, it is difficulty that rises with skill, plus short
  rounds.

## 6. Loops

- **Micro** (seconds): an action and its feedback. Press popoto, see the
  throw and the hug.
- **Meso** (a session or a day): today's popoto round; join a party, play,
  post the group photo.
- **Macro** (weeks or a season): a festival contest; prog through a tier;
  growing a collection; an Evercold month.

Healthy design nests them: each micro loop feeds a meso goal, and each meso
goal moves a macro story. A loop with no macro meaning gets dull; a macro
goal with no satisfying micro loop never starts. When adding a loop, attach
it to an existing one.

## 7. Progression and the player journey

The stages, and what each player needs from the site:

| Stage | Who | What they need |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | a new FC member, a guest | to understand the site in 30 seconds; one obvious first action |
| Onboarding | first day or week | claim and verify the character, send the first popoto, be greeted; a short "first day at the restaurant" path |
| Habit | regulars | a light daily ritual (the 07:00 popoto reset already is one) |
| Mastery | long-time members | depth: collections, showcase, contests, helping others |
| Elder | veterans, mentors, admins | status through *service*: mentoring, judging, writing flavour lines, hosting |
| Returning | "On vacation" (two thirds of the roster) | a warm welcome with no penalty for having left, and an easy re-entry |

Progression types:
- **Vertical** (levels, numbers going up) is easy to build, and inflates.
- **Horizontal** (new options and cosmetics) respects autonomy.
- **Social** (roles and responsibilities) creates community.

This site should lean on horizontal and social progression.

## 8. Octalysis (Yu-kai Chou): white hat versus black hat

Eight core drives:
1. Epic meaning
2. Accomplishment
3. Creativity and feedback
4. Ownership
5. Social influence and relatedness
6. Scarcity
7. Unpredictability
8. Loss and avoidance

- **Drives 1–3 are "white hat":** they make people feel good and in
  control.
- **Drives 6–8 are "black hat":** urgency and obsession that people resent
  afterwards. Use them in small doses, for fun moments only (a rare
  surprise), never as the backbone, and never for retention by fear.

## 9. The Hook model, used by halves

The model is a trigger, then an action, then a variable reward, then an
investment.

- **Keep** the investment half. Things the member builds (a showcase, a
  home, a collection, friendships) make the site theirs.
- **Keep** internal triggers that are real. "I want to thank my healer" is
  one; a push notification invented to pull people back is not.
- A variable reward is spice, not the meal.

## 10. Game feel ("juice")

Juice is the extra feedback that makes an action feel physical: anticipation
before, impact during, settle after. Squash and stretch, particles, sound,
screen shake. The popoto throw is the site's best example: an arc, a squash,
a hug, "+1", and the target's flinch.

The rules (fc-ui's motion.md has the details):
- Juice the actions people repeat.
- Make the rare thing bigger than the common one.
- Never delay the answer to a tap.
- Always respect reduced motion.
